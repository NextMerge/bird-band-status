Specialized
01 - Colored Leg Band: Plastic, Metal, Paint, Tape
Colored leg band(s) of plastic or metal - This applies to painted or anodized Federal bands as well as colored tape over bands. Note: two metal bands should never be used on the same tarsus.
02 - Neck Collar - Usually Coded
Neck collar - Collar codes and colors must be reported in marker-related fields.
03 - Reward Band (Federal or State)
Special reward bands used in recovery studies to incentivize public reporting of banded birds.
04 - Control Band (Reward Band Studies Only)
Control band - For use in conjunction with reward band studies only.
06 - Miscellaneous Metal Band with Contact Information
Metal bands with an additional address or telephone number, including State or Provincial bands, private organizations bands, and rarely banders. Explanation must be given in the Remarks field.
07 - Double-Banded (Two Federal Bands Simultaneously)
Two Federal bands placed on a bird at the same time. One Federal band on each tarsus --- two metal bands cannot be used on the same tarsus. This code does not apply to a bird to whom a second band was added at a subsequent encounter.
08 - Temporary Markers: Paint, Dye, Feather Modifications
Temporary markers - Any part of bird painted or dyed, or other temporary markers on feathers (e.g., imping, tail streamers, etc.).
09 - Flight Feathers Clipped or Pulled Upon Release
All flight feathers on one or both wings clipped or pulled upon release for temporary flight restriction.
11 - Sexed by Laparotomy or Laparoscopy
Surgical sexing procedures performed for species where external sexing is not reliable.
14 - Oral Swab Sample Collected
Mouth swab collected for genetic analysis, disease monitoring, or other research purposes.
16 - Tracheal Swab Sample Collected
Tracheal swab collected for respiratory disease monitoring and research.
18 - Blood Sample Collected
Blood sample taken (contact the appropriate Bird Banding Office for the required permit).
20 - Fostered or Cross-Fostered into Wild Nests
Birds that have been placed into wild nests as part of conservation or research programs.
